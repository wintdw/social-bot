Treatment: notebook diagram. For mechanism and structure — a system whose parts
talk to each other — where the *wiring* is the message. This is the one
treatment whose image is written as well as drawn.

Draw the idea as a hand-inked diagram on a page of a well-used notebook: two to
four simple rounded boxes and one central circle, joined by thick double-headed
arrows drawn freehand — the arrows are the conversation, so they curve and point
both ways. Each part carries its short label in plain capitals. Warm paper
ground with a faint spiral binding at the edge, a light watercolour wash of the
palette inside the shapes, ink outlines that are slightly imperfect, generous
white space around the diagram, a faint pencil construction line or two. The
feeling is a thought sketched in a notebook and kept: friendly, legible,
hand-made.

The labels are the only writing: no headings, no captions, no paragraphs, no
numbers, no legend. No people, no faces, no hands. No photograph, no 3D render,
no glossy UI mockup, no stock flowchart clip-art — this page was drawn by a
person, and the wobble in the lines is what says so.
